## Project Overview

This engineering concept explores how automation and process optimization could address service
inefficiencies in high-demand food-court restaurants. The project begins with a
business-performance problem: restaurant sales per square meter may be constrained when service
bottlenecks reduce customer turnover and cause potential customers to leave.

The proposed solution is an automated food-service module that combines principles of Industrial
Engineering and Mechatronics. The concept incorporates process standardization, operating-time
analysis, automation, robotics, and food-processing technologies to envision a faster and more
efficient service system.

The project was developed as a six-scene engineering storyboard, progressing from problem
identification to a conceptual automated solution and its projected effect on customer service and
sales performance.

</div>
</section>

<section class="section" markdown="1">
<div class="prose" markdown="1">

## Engineering Problem

Food-court restaurants may lose potential sales when service capacity is insufficient to meet
customer demand. Long waiting times can cause customers to leave, reducing customer turnover and
sales performance.

</div>

<div class="prose">
<ul class="cards">
  <li><strong>Performance indicator</strong> Sales revenue per square meter — V<sub>m²</sub> = total monthly sales revenue ÷ store area.</li>
  <li><strong>Current range</strong> USD 200–500 per m² per month.</li>
  <li><strong>Sector reference</strong> Up to USD 700–900 per m² per month.</li>
  <li><strong>Identified technical gap</strong> USD 200–700 per m² per month.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="prose" markdown="1">

<p class="note">The USD 300 per m² per month value shown in the final storyboard scene represents an
illustrative intermediate improvement scenario within the broader technical gap identified in the
project. It is not a measured result.</p>

</div>
</section>

<section class="section" markdown="1">
<div class="prose" markdown="1">

## Proposed Engineering Concept

The proposed concept is an automated food-service module designed to reduce operational bottlenecks
and increase service capacity.

From an Industrial Engineering perspective, the concept focuses on standardizing unit processes,
establishing operating times, and improving production flow. From a Mechatronics perspective, it
incorporates automation, electrical and mechanical systems, and programmable equipment.

The storyboard explores how technologies such as robotic handling, automated assembly concepts,
refrigeration, and food-processing equipment could be integrated into a future food-service system.

</div>
</section>

<section class="section" markdown="1">
<div class="prose" markdown="1">

## Engineering Technologies Explored

</div>

<div class="prose">
<ul class="cards">
  <li><strong>Flexible Manufacturing</strong> Process automation, variable-frequency-drive systems, pneumatic modules, and robotic handling using equipment such as the Motoman HP3 robot.</li>
  <li><strong>Smart Factory</strong> IoT technologies, collaborative robotics, and assembly-island concepts for automated production environments.</li>
  <li><strong>Unit Operations &amp; Food Processing</strong> Refrigeration and freeze-drying technologies considered in relation to food conservation and processing.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section" markdown="1">
<div class="prose" markdown="1">

## Scope &amp; Limitations

This project is a conceptual engineering storyboard rather than a physically constructed and
experimentally validated automated food-service system. The proposed technologies illustrate how
automation and process-engineering principles could potentially address the identified service
bottleneck.

The sales-per-square-meter values were used as reference indicators for defining the problem and
illustrating the potential business impact. The project did not experimentally demonstrate that the
proposed module would produce the projected sales improvement.

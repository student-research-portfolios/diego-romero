## Project Overview

This independent research project evaluated how the mass of thermally activated coconut-shell
biochar affected the residual concentration of copper(II) ions in a gravity-driven continuous-flow
filtration system. The experiment used 150 mL of copper sulfate pentahydrate solution at 0.05 mol/L
and tested three biochar masses: 1.0, 3.0, and 5.0 ± 0.01 g. Residual copper concentration was
estimated using colorimetric test strips. The reported mean residual concentrations were 6.50 mg/L
at 1.00 g, 4.17 mg/L at 3.00 g, and 3.83 mg/L at 5.00 g.

</div>
</section>

<section class="section" markdown="1">
<div class="prose" markdown="1">

## Research Question

<p class="callout">What is the effect of varying the mass of thermally activated coconut-shell
biochar (1.0, 3.0, and 5.0 ± 0.01 g) on the residual concentration of copper(II) ions (Cu²⁺),
estimated using colorimetric test strips, from a 0.05 mol/L copper sulfate pentahydrate
(CuSO₄·5H₂O) solution through gravity filtration?</p>

</div>
</section>

<section class="section" markdown="1">
<div class="prose" markdown="1">

## Context

The research frames copper contamination in relation to sources such as illegal and informal mining
and copper-based agricultural fungicides, and examines coconut-shell biochar as an accessible
material for water purification.

<p class="note">The experiment used a prepared copper sulfate pentahydrate solution. It did not test
actual mining or agricultural wastewater.</p>

</div>
</section>

<p class="note">Biochar preparation: drying and carbonization of coconut shell, crushing, sodium
bicarbonate treatment, heating, citric-acid washing, distilled-water washing, and final drying.</p>

<section class="section" markdown="1">
<div class="prose" markdown="1">

## Key Finding

The reported results show a decreasing trend in residual Cu²⁺ concentration as the mass of
coconut-shell biochar increased. The mean residual concentration decreased from 6.50 mg/L at
1.00 g to 4.17 mg/L at 3.00 g and to 3.83 mg/L at 5.00 g. The report states that the relationship
was not strictly linear and that the change from 3.00 g to 5.00 g was smaller, with an average
difference of 0.34 mg/L.

</div>
</section>

<section class="section" markdown="1">
<div class="prose" markdown="1">

## Interpretation

Increasing the biochar mass provides a greater contact surface and greater availability of active
micropores for trapping metal ions. The report suggests that, from 3.00 g onward, the drip-flow
system may begin to approach a state close to adsorption equilibrium for the 150 mL volume used.

The estimated effluent pH increased as biochar mass increased. The report discusses this in
relation to the sodium bicarbonate activation, the citric-acid washing, and Cu²⁺ retention.

</div>
</section>

<section class="section" markdown="1">
<div class="prose" markdown="1">

## Limitations

The central limitation identified in the report is the use of colorimetric test strips to estimate
Cu²⁺ concentration and pH. The report describes this method as semiquantitative and susceptible to
subjective visual interpretation. It proposes that future replications could use a
spectrophotometer and a digital pH meter, test wider biochar-mass intervals, and use different
copper sulfate concentrations.

</div>
</section>
